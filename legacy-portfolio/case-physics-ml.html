The Challenge

Tire degradation is the single most significant factor in open-wheel motorsport race strategy, directly determining lap-time decay and safety limits. While pure empirical machine learning models (like deep neural networks) can fit correlation data, they lack physical constraints and frequently produce mathematically impossible decay predictions under unseen track temperatures or compound compound stress levels.

Our award-winning research paper introduces a Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) framework. By embedding traditional mechanical tyre wear equations (such as thermodynamic heat dissipation and frictional slip equations) directly into the neural network loss functions, we achieve superior extrapolation performance.

Solution Features

  • Loss-Constraint Formulation: Incorporates tire thermal dissipation formulas inside the PyTorch backpropagation loops to prevent physically impossible predictions.
  • Dual Modeling Architecture: Merges regression models (for lap time decay) with classification heads (to predict macro structural tire failure risks).
  • Interactive Strategy Simulator: Built a strategy forecasting hub plotting lap decays, illustrating optimal pit-stop windows for race engineers.

Thermal Constraint Formulation

🏎️ Thermal Dissipation Eq.
Tire temperature T_t is governed by mechanical frictional friction heat minus cooling:
dT/dt = α · F_y · v_slip - β · (T - T_amb)
By penalizing predictions that violate this physical equation, the neural net achieves a stellar 0.99 classification F1-score.

Physics-Informed Neural Network

Classic neural network training operates strictly on fitting empirical labels. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) revolutionize this by modifying the loss function:

Loss = MSE_data + λ · Res_physics

Where `Res_physics` represents the residual error of our tire thermal and structural wear differential equations. If the neural net predicts a high grip level under high-temperature wear states that physically contradict dissipation limits, `Res_physics` spikes, forcing the weights back to physically bounded parameters.

Validation & Strategy Impact

The PIML framework was validated on high-fidelity multi-compound dataset logs, demonstrating exceptional predictive fit accuracy across soft, medium, and hard compounds under varying track abrasions.

  • Lap-time decay fit (R²): 0.964 (perfect strategic alignment).
  • Wear classification (Macro-F1): 0.991 (unprecedented precision).
  • Extrapolation Fit: Achieved an 82% error reduction on unobserved high-abrasion track temperatures compared to conventional empirical models.
