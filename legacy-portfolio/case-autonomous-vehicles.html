The Challenge

Autonomous vehicles must process complex environmental feeds in real time, detecting obstacles and predicting trajectory paths synchronously to avoid collisions. Traditional single-layer deep learning networks struggle to maintain high frame-rates while performing both detection and sequential path-forecasting.

This paper, published at IJCER 2024, outlines a dual-layer hybrid intelligence network. By merging object detection grids with sequential Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) layers, we achieve high-frequency telemetry tracking that operates with 87% trajectory prediction accuracy.

Solution Features

  • Optimized Object Isolation: Custom-trained YOLOv8 algorithms process active video streams, drawing tight bounding boxes around pedestrians, cars, and lane markers.
  • LSTM Horizon Forecasting: Decoupled sequential recurrent networks model the trajectory coordinate states of all detected objects over a 200ms future window.
  • Bandit-Optimized Decisions: Integrates a Multi-Armed Bandit model to dynamically govern priority focal zones, conserving computational resources.

Marquee Formulation

🤖 LSTM Recurrent State
The LSTM sequential update cell calculates hidden states h_t to map trajectories:
f_t = σ(W_f · [h_t-1, x_t] + b_f)
This mathematically filters past G-force coordinates to predict the path coordinates of nearby vehicles at 45 frames per second.

Network Pipeline Design

The network utilizes a highly parallelized pipeline structure. The camera feed is handled by an OpenCV capture loop, where frames are instantly resized and fed to PyTorch YOLOv8 weights.

Rather than passing the entire high-resolution frame into sequential forecasting, our design extracts the centroid coordinate offsets of detected bounding boxes. This micro-coordinate vector is fed into the LSTM layer, drastically reducing computing overhead and enabling real-time frame rates on consumer-grade GPUs.

Validation & Research Results

The model was tested extensively under synthetic simulated conditions and real-world video telemetry captures. In all scenarios, the model processed feeds under 22.2 milliseconds, comfortably beating target metrics.

  • Inference speed: 45 Frames Per Second (average).
  • Trajectory Tracking: 87% overall coordinate prediction accuracy under extreme lane-change transient tests.
  • Hardware footprint: CPU load remained under 15% due to our decoupled coordinate-pass strategy.

Recruiter Impact

By decoupling visual classification from trajectory modeling, this architecture proves that deep-learning decision systems can run on affordable edge processors, making it highly viable for next-gen production ADAS suites.
